\subsection{Performance Budgets}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Games run at 30--60 frames per second or more; AI gets only a small
        share of each frame~\cite{nareyek2004}.
  \item Common tricks: update distant NPCs less often, spread work across
        frames, use simpler AI for background characters (``level of
        detail'' for AI)~\cite{millington2009}.
  \item This explains why expensive techniques are often limited to a few
        important characters.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Designer Control versus Autonomy}
\begin{itemize}
  \item More autonomous AI (planning, learning) can surprise players in
        good ways, but also in bad ways that break levels or
        stories~\cite{orkin2006}.
  \item Designers need predictable results to build missions and
        balance difficulty.
  \item Most studios therefore choose ``authored autonomy'': AI decides
        within boundaries set by designers.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Believability versus Optimality}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The best-playing AI is not the most fun: perfect aim or perfect
        knowledge feels like cheating~\cite{yannakakis2018}.
  \item NPCs are often intentionally flawed: they miss first shots, announce
        their actions out loud, or hesitate.
  \item Believability can be tested with ``Turing tests'' for bots, such as
        the BotPrize competition~\cite{hingston2009}.
  \item The more human-like a character looks, the higher player
        expectations rise; small errors then feel uncanny~\cite{mori2012}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Development and Debugging}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Visual, inspectable systems (FSMs, behavior trees) are easier to
        debug and are favored in large teams~\cite{colledanchise2018}.
  \item Planners and learned models are harder to explain: it is not always
        clear \emph{why} an NPC chose an action.
  \item Tooling and editor support often decide which technique a studio
        adopts, more than raw capability.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Discussion}
\begin{itemize}
  \item No single technique wins on all criteria; the right choice depends
        on genre, team size and the role of the NPC.
  \item The trend is toward hybrids that combine designer-friendly
        structure with local autonomy.
  \item These trade-offs frame the current experiments discussed next.
\end{itemize}
